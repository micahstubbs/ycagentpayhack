## Overview

It’s clear that the future of the web is agentic.

But for agents to be truly web-native, they’ll need to pay for things. This might mean ordering parts to complete a physical repair, paying another agent for specialized research, orchestrating contractor work, or making micropayments to access private data.

In this hackathon, you’ll build agents with spending power - autonomous actors that can manipulate economic resources.

This space is heating up quickly. Several of our sponsors have recently announced that they’re building rails for agentic payments. But things are still early, which means the upside will never be greater - build with us now and shape the future!

### Description

Build something awesome with Locus - the payment infrastructure for AI agents. Whether you're creating fully autonomous agents, human-in-the-loop systems, or entirely new payment experiences, we want to see how you leverage programmable payments for AI.

Locus isn't just rails - it's Ramp for agents, giving you granular control over how AI spends money. The best projects will thoughtfully use spending controls, permissions, and workflows to enable powerful agent capabilities safely.

Possible directions (but don't limit yourself to these!):

- **Autonomous agents** that make independent spending decisions to accomplish goals
- **Agent-to-agent economies** where AI systems pay each other for services, data, or compute
- **AI-powered financial tools** that help humans make better payment decisions
- **Novel payment flows** enabled by instant, programmable transactions
- **Multi-agent coordination** using payments as a coordination mechanism
- **Prediction market trading agents** that use Kalshi's new announcement of USDC

We're excited to see creative applications across any domain - research, productivity, commerce, infrastructure, entertainment, or something we haven't thought of yet. The key is demonstrating how Locus's payment rails enable new capabilities.

### Judging Criteria

Projects will be evaluated across 6 categories:

1. **Innovation (20%)** - How novel is the use case? Does this demonstrate a new capability enabled by agent payments?
2. **Technical Execution (20%)** - Is the integration clean? Does the agent make payments reliably?
3. **Practical Value (20%)** - Would people/businesses actually use this? Does it solve a real problem?
4. **Use of Locus (15%)** - Is this an impressive application of Locus's technology? Could this be extended and developed further in the future?
5. **Agent Autonomy (15%)** - How much genuine decision-making does the agent do? Are the payment decisions contextually intelligent?
6. **Demo Quality (10%)** - Was there a real demo for the project? Was it showcased live and effectively?

# Rules

## Eligibility & Participation:

1. All participants must be physically present at YC HQ for the duration of the event. Remote participation is not permitted.
2. All code must be written during the event (after 9:00 AM on 11/15). We will review GitHub commit history to verify compliance.
3. You may use existing libraries, frameworks, and APIs, but the core project must be built during the hackathon.
4. Teams can be 1-4 people. Form teams before hacking begins or find teammates during the opening ceremony.
5. Each person can only be on one team.

## Submissions:

1. The submission deadline is **5:00 PM sharp**. No exceptions - late submissions will be disqualified.
2. Projects can be submitted to multiple tracks, but must meet each track's specific requirements.
3. Each submission must include:
   1. Working code,
   2. A brief demo video or live website, and
   3. A short description of what the project does and how it uses the relevant technologies.
4. Your code repository must be public or shared with organizers by the submission deadline.

## Resources & Support:

1. Locus will provide each participant/team with $10 USDC in wallet credits to start. Need more for testing? Find a Locus team member (we'll be wearing Locus merch).
2. Sponsor representatives will be available during the workshops (1:30-3:30 PM) and throughout the day for technical questions.
3. WiFi and power outlets will be available. Bring your own laptop and charger.

## Judging:

1. Judges’ decisions are final. Projects will be evaluated based on each track’s specific criteria.
2. You must be present to demo your project during judging (5:05 PM onward).
3. Winners must be present at closing ceremony to claim respective prizes.
4. Each sponsor/track will coordinate with their respective winners to distribute their specific prize.

## Venue & Conduct:

1. Y Combinator is generously hosting us - please help keep the space clean. Clean up after yourself, use trash/recycling bins, and respect the venue.
2. Be respectful to all attendees, sponsors, organizers, and the venue. Harassment, discrimination, or disruptive behaviour will result in immediate disqualification and removal from the event.
3. Food and beverages are provided, but please be mindful of others - don't hoard food or create waste.
4. Photography and videography may occur during the event for promotional purposes. Let an organizer know if you prefer not to be photographed.
5. Upon registration, you will be given a wristband to wear during the event. Please ensure that you do not remove this wristband under any circumstances as our security uses it to validate your attendance as a hacker for the event.
6. Do not enter any rooms or areas of the venue marked by “YC Staff Only” (this includes for example: conference rooms, the 2nd floor, etc)

## Intellectual Property:

1. You retain all rights to your code and ideas. By participating, you agree to let Locus and sponsors mention your project in promotional materials (with attribution).
2. Ensure you have the right to use any third-party code, assets, or APIs in your project.

## Safety & Fair Play:

1. No projects that promote harm, illegal activities, hate speech, or violate the terms of service of the platforms/APIs being used.
2. Any form of cheating (including pre-written code beyond standard libraries) will result in disqualification.

## Presenting to Judges:

1. Depending on which track(s) you submit to, you will need to make sure you present to that respective track’s judge during the allotted judging period. For submission to the Locus tracks, attendees will be provided a number from 1-5 for their team, and further a sub-number from 1-20. This will determine which table as well as judging time you present at.
   1. For example, “1:1” corresponds to presenting first at table 1, meaning you will need to be ready at 5:05 to present to judge table 1
   2. For other sponsor tracks, each will have their own dedicated judging table and will judge on a rolling basis. Be sure to find the chance to present.
